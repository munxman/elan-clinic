How Élan Clinic Works

From first consultation to long-term maintenance — a structured, physician-led process.

Book a Consultation 45 minutes · remote or in-clinic · Tallinn

Regain Shield is built for the phase most systems ignore: what happens after appetite suppression ends. The process is deliberately clinical: we measure baseline physiology, design a personalised protocol, and keep adjusting it as your markers evolve.

The 4-Step Process

A maintenance system — not generic advice

The homepage summary is accurate. Here is what it looks like in practice.

01

Initial Consultation (45 minutes)

A physician-led consultation to understand your GLP‑1 history, current reality, and maintenance goals. We map what changed during the medication phase — and what is likely to change once dose is reduced or stopped.

  • Format: remote (secure video) or in-clinic (Tallinn).
  • What happens: structured history, risk review, medication timeline, and a clear plan for next steps.
  • What to prepare: current/previous GLP‑1 dose, other meds, recent labs (if available), weight history, and your target maintenance range.
  • What you’ll learn: the likely drivers of regain risk in your case, which programme fits, and what we will measure first.
Output: a written next-step plan for assessment + the initial monitoring schedule.
02

Clinical Assessment

Before designing anything, we establish your baseline physiology. Maintenance is a different clinical phase than weight loss — and assumptions here are expensive.

  • Baseline bloodwork: metabolic markers, lipids, liver/kidney function, thyroid, inflammation, and targeted additions based on history.
  • Body composition: lean mass vs fat mass assessment (programme-dependent; DEXA where indicated).
  • Metabolic profiling: patterns in glucose control, insulin dynamics, and risk markers relevant to your regain trajectory.
  • What we track: trendlines over time — not one-off numbers.
Output: your baseline “maintenance dashboard” — what we’re monitoring, how often, and what thresholds trigger adjustments.
03

Protocol Design

Your protocol is engineered around your baseline data and your post‑GLP‑1 physiology. The aim is to defend results, protect lean mass, and reduce the rebound mechanisms that drive regain.

  • Nutrition: personalised parameters built for satiety, adherence, and maintenance energy balance.
  • Resistance training: progressive plan designed to preserve or rebuild lean mass (the “hidden variable” in many regains).
  • Medication management (if needed): physician-led decisions; tapered continuity or alternatives when clinically indicated.
  • Behavioural architecture: rebuilding the psychological scaffolding the medication provided (cues, structure, friction, and relapse prevention).
Output: a written protocol + cadence of check-ins + escalation triggers.
04

Ongoing Clinical Care

Maintenance is not a one-time plan. It’s a feedback loop. We keep measuring, reviewing, and adjusting before problems become visible on the scale.

  • Check-in cadence: monthly (Standard) with higher-frequency contact where appropriate.
  • Quarterly bloodwork: minimum standard; more frequent panels if markers drift or risk rises.
  • Protocol adjustments: nutrition/training/behaviour changes based on trendlines and adherence reality.
  • Escalation triggers: predefined thresholds (clinical + behavioural) that prompt action early.
Output: a living protocol that evolves with your physiology — not a PDF you never revisit.
Timeline

What the first months typically look like

Every patient is different. This is the usual structure — designed to stabilise early, then optimise, then maintain.

Month 1

Assessment

Initial consultation, baseline labs/body composition, risk map, and first protocol version.

Months 2–3

Stabilisation

Adherence engineering, hunger rebound management, routine building, early adjustments.

Months 4–6

Optimisation

Lean mass and performance focus, deeper marker improvements, tighter calibration of protocol.

Month 7+

Maintenance

Quarterly reviews, long-term guardrails, relapse prevention, and proactive escalation when needed.
