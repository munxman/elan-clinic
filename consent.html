Consultation Terms (What to Expect)

3.1 Nature of consultations

Consultations may be provided in-clinic and/or remotely (telemedicine) depending on your needs and what is clinically appropriate. A consultation typically includes history taking, review of relevant records and measurements, clinical assessment within the limits of the chosen format, discussion of options, and an agreed plan.

3.2 Patient responsibilities

To provide safe care, you agree to:

  • provide complete and accurate information about symptoms, medical history, medications, allergies, and relevant lifestyle factors;
  • inform us about changes in your condition and follow agreed-up monitoring and follow-up plans;
  • use the consultation for your own health needs only (not for third parties unless explicitly agreed and legally permitted);
  • treat clinic staff with respect; abusive behaviour may result in termination of services.

3.3 Scheduling, cancellation and rescheduling

If you need to reschedule or cancel an appointment, please do so as early as possible to allow the slot to be offered to other patients.

3.4 No-show policy

If you do not attend your appointment without notice (“no-show”), we may charge a no-show fee and/or require prepayment for future bookings. This is to protect clinician time and maintain access for other patients.

Practical rule of thumb: if you cannot attend, notify us as soon as possible. If you are unsure what fees apply to your booking, contact us before the appointment.

3.5 Clinical decisions and referrals

Clinical decisions are made based on information available at the time. We may recommend tests, follow-ups, or in-person assessment. Where appropriate, we may recommend referral to another clinician or service.

4

Informed Consent for Telemedicine / Remote Consultations

Telemedicine means providing clinical services remotely using audio/video communication and digital tools. By choosing a remote consultation, you confirm that you understand and accept the following.

4.1 What telemedicine can include

  • review of your medical history, symptoms, and goals;
  • review of test results and health data you provide;
  • discussion of treatment options, lifestyle protocols, and follow-up;
  • where legally and clinically appropriate, issuing prescriptions or documentation.

4.2 Identity, location, and contact details

For safety and legal compliance, we may need to verify your identity and confirm your physical location at the time of the consultation (for example, to determine what emergency services are available if needed).

4.3 Confidentiality and technical risks

We take reasonable steps to protect confidentiality. However, electronic communication involves risks, including potential technical failures, unauthorised access, or data transmission errors.

4.4 Recording

Remote consultations are not recorded unless we obtain explicit consent in advance and provide a clear purpose for recording.

4.5 Right to withdraw consent

You may withdraw consent for telemedicine at any time. If remote care is no longer appropriate, we may recommend in-person assessment or referral.

5

Limitations of Remote Medical Consultations

5.1 No physical examination (or limited exam)

Remote consultations may limit our ability to perform a physical examination and to observe clinical signs. This may affect diagnostic certainty.

5.2 Incomplete information

The quality of remote care depends on the completeness and accuracy of information you provide, and the availability of relevant measurements and test results.

5.3 When an in-person assessment is required

We may determine that an in-person consultation, urgent evaluation, or emergency care is required. If so, you agree to seek in-person care promptly.

5.4 Not suitable for emergencies

Telemedicine is not intended for emergencies or rapidly deteriorating conditions.
